 4. Core Analytical Methodology — Patient Site Month (PSM)

 Definition

 Site-level (PRIMARY for Ora/Buddy): site_psm = enrolled ÷ months(FSI → LSI from ora_veeva_milestone), min 1 month.
 Study-level rollup: median of positive site PSMs, or enrolled ÷ (n_sites × enrollment_months) when only study totals exist.
 TrialHub: use psm_common / th_actual_psm (study-level industry) — label grain when comparing to Ora site PSM.

 Study-Level PSM

 study_psm = median(site_psm) for the study when site milestones exist; else total_enrolled / (n_active_sites × enrollment_duration_months)
 Enrollment duration = last patient in minus first patient in, in months. Do NOT use planned window — use actuals.

 Site-Level PSM

 site_psm = site_enrolled / site_enroll_months
 site_enroll_months = months between site FSI milestone actual and site LSI milestone actual (same month → 1).
 Sites with 0 enrolled have PSM = 0, not null — activated but did not enroll; exclude from medians, still list.

 The ~80% Null PSM Rate

 Critical structural fact: Many site records lack FSI/LSI actuals and/or enrolled counts, so site_psm is null. Null ≠ zero. When calculating indication-level benchmarks, use the subset with positive PSM and note the n.

 Block Enrollment Studies

 Always exclude block enrollment studies from startup speed calculations. In block enrollment designs, the sponsor pre-selects patients before sites are formally activated — site-level activation timing is not independently measurable and will skew startup metrics. Identify block enrollment studies by examining the study design notes or by flagging studies where SIV-to-FSI is 0 days (impossible in normal enrollment). When in doubt, ask the project team.

 PSM Calculation — Forward-Looking vs Historical

 Historical PSM: Computed from actual Veeva data. Used for benchmarking and site scoring.
 Planning PSM: The assumption used to calculate sites needed for a proposed study. Always present planning PSM as a scenario input, not a fact. Standard approach: use historical Veeva median PSM for the indication and geography as the base assumption, then run sensitivity analysis across low/base/high scenarios.

 Sites Needed Formula

 sites_needed = target_enrolled / (planning_PSM × enrollment_months)
buffer_sites = sites_needed × 1.20 # 20% over-enrollment buffer standard
Example: 80 pts / (0.5 PSM × 12 months) = 13.3 sites → recommend 16 with buffer

 5. Benchmarking & Comparator Selection

 Priority Hierarchy for Benchmarks

 Always select benchmarks in this order, most preferred first:

 - Ora internal data (Veeva) — same indication, same geography, same phase. Most relevant but often limited sample size.

 - Ora internal data — adjacent — same indication, different geography OR same phase, adjacent indication (e.g., broad RP for a specific gene target).

 - TrialHub industry benchmarks — industry-sponsored studies in indication. Filter to same phase and similar geography. Compute implied PSM from pts/(sites×duration) where available.

 - CT.gov site-level data — when TrialHub lacks site breakdown. Use for OUS site identification and sponsor landscape.

 - Literature/assumption — only when no data exists. Flag clearly as assumed, not evidenced.

 Comparator Vetting Rules

 Screen fail rate from screened ÷ enrolled — only use if screen fail rate is between 5% and 80%. Rates outside this range suggest data entry errors (e.g., enrolled > screened, or screened entered as patients in the population not the study).

 Exclude studies with <3 enrolled patients from PSM benchmarks — insufficient to be meaningful.

 For nAMD: the historical Ora PSM of 0.065 reflects an era when anti-VEGF was new and patient identification was harder. Current standard planning PSM is 0.35. Never use the historical benchmark for planning without flagging this discrepancy to the sponsor.

 When sample size is small (n<5 PSM observations), widen the comparison to include adjacent indications or geographies and note the limitation explicitly in the report.

 Screen Fail Rate Defaults (when not in data)

 Indication / Context Default Screen Fail Rationale

 Dry Eye (general) 25–35% Large symptomatic population, sign/symptom criteria typically moderate

 Dry Eye (Sjögren's / aqueous deficient) 40–50% Specific autoimmune diagnosis required; referral network essential

 nAMD (active CNV) 30–40% Imaging criteria strict; active vs. quiescent distinction

 Glaucoma (established diagnosis) 30–35% IOP and visual field criteria; OCT progression req. adds complexity

 Early-stage DR (ERG criterion) 40–55% ERG neurodysfunction not routinely measured; novel screening criterion

 IRD / gene therapy 50–70% Genotype confirmation required; rare specific mutations

 TED (active, CAS ≥4) 35–45% Activity criterion; treatment-naive requirement typically

 PCED 30–40% Specific diagnosis; must have failed prior treatment

 EU general adjustment +5–10% vs US Stricter eligibility documentation requirements, slower pre-screening

 Dropout Rate Defaults

 Unless specified in the protocol: 10–15% for 12-month studies , 15–20% for 18–24 month studies , 8–12% for 3–6 month studies . Adjust upward for elderly populations, complex visit schedules, or studies with invasive procedures. Always present as an assumption and invite the sponsor to provide their own estimate.

 6. Enrollment Sensitivity Tables

 Every feasibility report must include an enrollment sensitivity table. This is not optional — it is one of the most valuable pieces of output for a sponsor because it shows the impact of PSM and site count variability on enrollment duration, helping them make resourcing decisions.

 Standard 3×3 Format

 Rows = PSM scenarios (Low / Base / High). Columns = site count scenarios (Conservative / Planned / Aggressive). Each cell = months to enroll. Highlight the base/planned cell. Color-code: ≤protocol window = green, within 20% over = amber, >20% over = red.

# Python template for sensitivity table
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

target_pts = 120
psm_scenarios = {'Low': 0.30, 'Base': 0.50, 'High': 0.70}
site_scenarios = {'Conservative (12)': 12, 'Planned (16)': 16, 'Aggressive (20)': 20}
protocol_window = 12 # months

rows = []
for psm_label, psm_val in psm_scenarios.items():
 row = {'PSM': f'{psm_label} ({psm_val})'}
 for site_label, n_sites in site_scenarios.items():
 months = round(target_pts / (psm_val * n_sites), 1)
 row[site_label] = f'{months} mo'
 rows.append(row)

df = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index('PSM')
print(df.to_string())

 What to Include Above the Sensitivity Table

 - Base PSM assumption and its source (Ora historical / TrialHub industry / assumed)

 - Planned site count and how it was derived

 - Protocol enrollment window for reference

 - Key eligibility factors that could affect PSM (novel criteria, rare population, etc.)

 Standard OUS Site Count Targets

 When an OUS expansion is needed, target 2–4 sites per priority country depending on enrollment contribution needed. Never recommend a country for 1 site only unless there is a specific strategic reason (KOL relationship, unique patient population) — regulatory activation overhead is rarely justified for 1 site. Australia is an exception: even 1–2 Australian sites add speed value due to the CTN pathway.

 "Initiated By" Clarification

 Critical terminology rule: In protocols, "trial initiated" or "study start" typically refers to regulatory submission / study activation — NOT first patient first visit (FPFV). FPFV happens after the startup phase completes (typically 3–6 months post-initiation). Always clarify with the sponsor which date they mean, and report startup assumptions separately from enrollment assumptions.

 Report Flexibility Rule

 Claude must be able to receive specs in two modes: (1) Chat spec — user describes the ask in natural language; Claude extracts the relevant parameters and proceeds. (2) RFP file ingestion — user uploads a protocol PDF, Word doc, or email; Claude reads the file, extracts all relevant specs, lists what was found and what is missing, and proceeds or asks targeted questions for the gaps only. Never ask for information already present in the uploaded file. Never refuse to proceed because a spec is missing — use best-practice defaults and flag assumptions clearly.

 RFP File Extraction Checklist

 When a protocol or RFP is uploaded, extract and confirm the following before building the report:

 - Indication and specific subtype (e.g., uveitis — anterior vs posterior)

 - Phase and study design (randomized, masked, parallel, crossover)

 - Sample size (total enrolled) and randomization ratio

 - Enrollment window (months) vs total study duration (months)

 - Target geographies (countries, scenarios if multiple)

 - Number of sites requested (if specified — often Ora is asked to propose)

 - Key eligibility criteria that affect enrollment (novel/restrictive criteria, genotype requirements, prior treatment washout)

 - Primary endpoint (affects site capability requirements)

 - Special equipment / procedures required (ERG, OCT-A, microperimetry, etc.)

 - "Initiated by" date — clarify if regulatory start or FPFV

 - Screen fail and dropout assumptions (use defaults if not specified)

 10. Sponsor-Facing vs Internal Rules

 These rules are non-negotiable and must be applied to every sponsor-facing document without exception. Internal documents (BD targets, site performance, ELT decks) are exempt from these rules and may cite data sources and use internal terminology.

 Sponsor-Facing Document Rules (Feasibility Reports, Site Recommendation Reports)

 NO data source citations. Never mention CT.gov, ClinicalTrials.gov, TrialHub, Veeva, UCSD, FWA numbers, or any specific database in sponsor-facing documents. Present findings as Ora's proprietary intelligence — which it is.

 NO 100% screen-to-enroll language. Never imply every screened patient will enroll. Always present enrollment as a projection with screen fail assumptions stated. Phrases like "all screened patients are expected to enroll" are never acceptable.

 NO study names. Never name specific Ora studies (e.g., "LASN01-CL-2201") in sponsor-facing documents. Use generic references: "a recent Ora Phase IIa study in this indication."

 NO multi-location language unless confirmed. Do not say a site "has multiple locations" or "operates across X cities" unless this has been independently verified. Vance Thompson Vision is a known example — do not mention multiple locations without confirmation.

 NO PI credentials that are unverified. If a PI's credential (MD vs OD) is uncertain, omit the credential rather than guess. Known corrections: Nancy Cline = MD (not OD); Eugene McLaurin = MD (not OD).

 Forward-looking language: use "Potential Phase X" not "Phase X needed." The distinction matters — "needed" implies certainty about the sponsor's plans, which may not be accurate or appropriate.

 Recently completed framing: Use "Sponsor may have data and may be planning next study now." Do NOT say "Sponsor needs a Phase X" or "Sponsor is planning a Phase X." Ora does not know the sponsor's internal plans.

 11. BD Copy Conventions

 BD Completion Target Report Logic

 The BD completion targets report identifies sponsors with studies completing in the near future (typically a 12-month forward window) and flags them as BD opportunities. Logic:

 - Forward-looking: Use harmonized TrialHub data (Ora-scoped indications only). Filter to primary completion date within the target window. Flag as "Potential Phase X" (next phase after completing).

 - Recently completed: Use raw TrialHub data (not Ora-scoped). Studies with primary completion in the past 0–6 months. Frame as "Sponsor may have data and may be planning next study now."

 - 4D Molecular note: 4D Molecular Therapeutics is NOT a current Ora client despite appearing in some datasets. Do not classify as "Has Wins."

 13. Veeva Query Safety Rules

 Partial organization name searches without geographic filters produce cross-site contamination. This is a known failure mode that has caused errors in prior analyses. These rules are mandatory.

 Rule 1 — Always filter org name + state + city. Never aggregate data across sites using organization name alone. A search for "Eye Care Associates" without state/city filtering returns sites in multiple states that are unrelated practices. Every site query must validate against organization name AND state AND city before aggregating.

 Rule 2 — Print distinct organizations before aggregating. Before summing any metrics (enrolled, PSM, etc.) across multiple site records, print the distinct organization names and cities to confirm they represent the intended sites. This takes 2 lines of code and catches false matches before they corrupt the output.

 Rule 3 — Handle multi-location organizations explicitly. If a search returns records for an organization across multiple cities (e.g., Vance Thompson Vision in Sioux Falls SD and West Fargo ND), treat each city as a separate site. Do not merge without intent. Flag multi-location organizations in the output.

 Rule 4 — Validate returned records before analysis. After any site query, spot-check that the returned records make sense for the intended analysis. If unexpected organizations appear, investigate before including.

 16. Indication-Specific Intelligence

 Uveitis — Critical Subtype Rules

 NEVER report uveitis enrollment or PSM data without specifying the anatomic subtype. Anterior, intermediate, posterior, and panuveitis require different sites, investigators, and have dramatically different enrollment profiles.

 Subtype Also known as Enrollment profile Key site type

 Anterior uveitis Iritis, iridocyclitis, JIA-associated uveitis, HLA-B27 uveitis Most common — relatively easy to enroll. High prevalence, many anterior segment sites can screen. Anterior segment / comprehensive ophthalmology

 Intermediate uveitis Pars planitis Less common. Requires vitreous involvement confirmation. Uveitis specialists, retina/vitreous

 Posterior uveitis Choroiditis, chorioretinitis, retinitis Rare, severe. Requires specialized uveitis centres with retinal imaging capability. Much lower PSM than anterior. Dedicated uveitis centres, academic medical centres

 Panuveitis All compartments involved Rarest and most severe subtype. Very low PSM. Only a handful of sites nationally can run these studies. Academic uveitis subspecialty centres only

 NIU Non-infectious uveitis — a broad catch-all term Highly variable — depends entirely on which anatomic subtypes are eligible per protocol. Always confirm subtype before pulling benchmarks. Confirm protocol before site selection

 Never use anterior uveitis trials (e.g., JIA-associated, HLA-B27) as benchmarks for posterior or panuveitis studies. The patient populations are completely different.

 nAMD — PSM Planning Rule

 Use 0.35 PSM for nAMD planning. Ora's historical Veeva median PSM for nAMD is ~0.065 — this reflects a historical era when anti-VEGF was novel and enrollment was harder. Current industry benchmarks (Roche, Regeneron, Novartis Phase 3 studies) achieve PSM of 0.3–0.5 at established retina centres. Never use 0.065 as a planning assumption without flagging the discrepancy to the sponsor and explaining why a higher planning PSM is appropriate.

 Stargardt's Disease

 32 competing industry trials active globally as of mid-2026. Patient pool is genuinely limited — the same patients cycle through multiple studies. PSM of 0.12–0.16 is realistic. Managing sponsor enrollment expectations early is essential for this indication. Key sites: Bascom Palmer (Lam/Gregori), Johns Hopkins, Massachusetts Eye and Ear, Moorfields (UK), CERA (Australia). Natural history study eligibility (ABCA4 biallelic mutations confirmed) is a must-have site capability.

 Thyroid Eye Disease (TED)

 Ora ran LASN01-CL-2201 (Lassen Therapeutics Phase IIa): 46 sites, US/UK/Spain/Germany, 41 enrolled. Key US enrollers: Oester (Wilmington Eye, 9 enrolled), Leibowitz (Las Vegas, 8 enrolled), Kossler (Stanford, 3 enrolled). Key OUS enrollers: Garrido (Seville, 5 enrolled — top OUS), Kahaly (Mainz — leading European TED/Graves' researcher), Malik (Moorfields). TED requires oculoplastics/orbital specialists — NOT general ophthalmology sites. Sites must have endocrinology referral relationships for the Graves' disease patient pipeline. CAS scoring, proptosis measurement (Hertel exophthalmometry), and diplopia assessment are required endpoint capabilities.

 PCED (Persistent Corneal Epithelial Defect)

 Primary endpoint is ALWAYS complete corneal epithelial closure measured by fluorescein staining = 0mm². "Improvement" or "reduction" language is not acceptable for PCED studies — regulators require complete closure. Sites must have anterior segment surgeons with PCED experience and high-resolution anterior segment OCT. Patient population: post-surgical, neurotrophic keratopathy, chemical burns, severe dry eye with PED. Rare condition — expect low PSM (0.15–0.25).

 Early-Stage Diabetic Retinopathy (ERG-Defined Neurodysfunction)

 A novel patient identification criterion requiring RETeval portable ERG to confirm retinal neurodysfunction before enrollment. Critical caveats: (1) most retina centers do not routinely perform ERG screening — sites must be selected for this capability or trained; (2) ETDRS ≤47 (mild/no clinical retinopathy) means patients look normal on fundus exam — primary care / endocrinology referral pipelines are as important as ophthalmology site databases; (3) PSM expectations should be 0.05–0.10 for this specific population, much lower than standard DME studies; (4) the EUROCONDOR study (NCT01726075, Rafael Simó / Cristina Hernández at Vall d'Hebron Barcelona) is the closest historical comparator — 43 centres, Spain/Italy/Portugal/UK/Germany/Denmark, 449 subjects, ~3 years. This implies PSM of ~0.13 but over a long enrollment window.

 Inherited Retinal Diseases (IRD) / Gene Therapy

 Key distinctions: (1) PSM is heavily dependent on the specific genetic mutation — RPGR (X-linked RP) has a larger patient pool than CEP290 LCA10 which has a very small pool; (2) genotype confirmation by molecular testing is required before enrollment in all gene therapy studies — sites must have genetic counseling or an established testing workflow; (3) natural history studies feed the gene therapy pipeline — sites with ongoing natural history registries are the strongest candidates; (4) ProQR (now Sepul) operates in LCA10 (sepofarsen) and USH2A (ultevursen) — these are separate programmes. In Ora Veeva, "ProQR LCA" = the LCA10/sepofarsen programme coded as "Leber congenital amaurosis." There is no confirmed USH2A-specific Ora study in Veeva. (5) Beijing Tongren Eye Center (PI: Prof. Zi-Bing Jin) is the leading IRD site in Beijing, with 9 active CT.gov IRD studies. Wenbin Wei, PhD is the active interventional PI. Ora has 1 study at Beijing Tongren (OcuMension OT-101, pediatric myopia — different department from IRD).

 Glaucoma Neuroprotection

 A growing indication with renewed industry interest after the failure of memantine in Phase 3. Key endpoint complexity: RNFL and GCL thinning on OCT require documented baseline and progression data — sites need access to patients with established glaucoma and longitudinal OCT records. Visual field progression, ERG, microperimetry, and contrast sensitivity are secondary endpoints in some protocols — confirm site equipment capability. PSM is typically lower than IOP-lowering glaucoma studies because the progressive RNFL criterion limits the eligible pool. Planning PSM: 0.25–0.40 US, 0.15–0.30 EU. Startup note: neuroprotection studies typically have 18–24 month total duration (8 months enrollment + 12 months follow-up) — enrollment window is meaningful for PSM calculation.

 Usher Syndrome / USH2A

 No confirmed Usher-specific studies in Ora Veeva. 10 RP studies (40 sites, 140 enrolled) may include USH2A patients under broad RP eligibility criteria but are coded as "Retinitis Pigmentosa." If a sponsor asks about Usher experience, the honest answer is: Ora has deep RP experience with investigators who routinely see Usher patients (Pennesi at OHSU, Fahim at Kellogg, Huckfeldt at Mass Eye and Ear) but no study coded specifically as Usher in our database. Active industry USH2A programmes include AAVantgarde Bio (CELESTA), Nacuity, Laboratoires Thea. 9 total industry Usher trials in TrialHub.
